# Output↔taste alignment — does Mark write what he loves?

**Origin (recovered):** The `D:/Dropbox/audio` steward, building a re-sing/DJ substrate over Mark's own songs, found Mark scrobbles his own recordings (artist "Mark Koranda", 1014 plays / 147 tracks, his #15 most-played artist, concentrated 2009–2015). It filed a two-part v1 bridge (`from_audio_self-catalog-bridge_2026-05-30.md`). Part (A) — pull per-song self-play counts into the audio substrate as an affinity signal — needs nothing from us (audio reads `music.db` read-only) and is already served on our side by `self_affinity.py`. Part (B), still open, is the ask below. Audio's verbatim framing: "*does Mark write what he loves?* — compare the audio-feature centroid of his top-played *artists* (Muse / Radiohead / The National / Mason Jennings) against the feature centroid of his *own* catalog. If your model can ingest content features, this is a novel axis (output↔taste alignment, drift over time). If it's consumption-only by design, ignore."

**The ask (in this project's terms):** The engine currently models taste purely from *consumption* (Last.fm/Spotify play behavior). Part B asks whether to extend it to *content features*: ingest `audiomemory.db`'s acoustic vectors (key, tempo, chroma, MFCC, spectral), compute a feature centroid for Mark's top-played artists and a centroid for his own catalog, and measure their distance — an "output matches taste" axis, with an optional drift-over-time view. The first move is a design decision: is content-feature ingestion in-scope for this engine, or is it consumption-only by design?

**In-scope core / drift split (ADR-0003):** Firmly ours — the taste/consumption side (`music.db`), the centroid-over-top-played-artists computation, and the in/out scope decision for content features. Handled-as-consumer (audio owns it) — the `sw#### ↔ scrobble-track ↔ playcount` fuzzy-mapping (audio offered to own this and hand over a clean table) and `audiomemory.db` feature extraction. Do not re-derive the rosetta; consume audio's table.

**htttw service:** Weak-to-moderate. A defensible output↔taste-alignment metric is a small rigor/novelty contribution (a real comparison method on real data); but it serves one bounded analysis, not the terminal's core argument, so scored 2 not 3. Score is contingent — if the design decision is "consumption-only, decline," htttw_contribution effectively drops toward 0 (no method produced).
